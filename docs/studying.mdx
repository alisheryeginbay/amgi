Keys while studying: <kbd>Space</kbd> shows the answer, <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>4</kbd> rate
Again/Hard/Good/Easy ([or keys you choose](#changing-the-answer-keys)), <kbd>⌘Z</kbd>
takes back the last rating and shows that card again, <kbd>R</kbd> replays the card's
sounds, <kbd>⌘O</kbd> opens a folder and <kbd>⌘R</kbd> reloads. Files also reload
whenever the app comes to the front.

**View ▸ Command Palette** (<kbd>⌘K</kbd>) finds any command as you type: <kbd>↑</kbd>
and <kbd>↓</kbd> pick one, <kbd>Return</kbd> runs it and <kbd>Esc</kbd> puts the
palette away. What you can do with the card on screen comes
first, with each command's shortcut beside it.

A card that asks you to [type its answer](/card-format#notes-with-fields) has a box
under the question: type, then press <kbd>Return</kbd> to see the answer with what you
typed checked against it. Rate it with <kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>4</kbd> as usual.

The header counts what's left today: **due** cards come back after
[days](#when-cards-are-due), **learning** cards are ones you answered **Again** and
haven't passed since, and **new** cards you haven't studied. Once some reviews are
[timed](#time-spent-studying), it also says about how long they'll take. Which of these
the card on screen is shows just above it.

The window studies the whole collection. To find cards, see a card's history or your
statistics, use the [command line](/cli): `amgi search`, `amgi info` and `amgi stats`.

## Changing the answer keys

The keys that rate a card can be changed in `~/.config/amgi/config.toml` (or under
`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME` when that's set). The file belongs to you rather than a collection,
so the keys stay the same whichever folder you open.

```toml ~/.config/amgi/config.toml
[keys]
again = "j"
hard = "k"
good = "l"
easy = ";"
```

Each is one character; a grade left out keeps its number. <kbd>Space</kbd> and
<kbd>R</kbd> are taken, and two grades can't share a key. The file is read again when
the app comes to the front or you press <kbd>⌘R</kbd>; if it has a mistake, Amgi says
what and keeps the keys it had. **Help ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts** shows the keys in use.

## When cards are due

A card you remember comes back a whole number of days later, and is due for that whole
day. A card you forget (**Again**) comes back after 10 minutes; when nothing else is
left, it is shown up to 20 minutes early. Cards from the next day never are.

A new day starts at 4 AM, so studying after midnight counts as the day before. Change the
hour in **Settings** (<kbd>⌘,</kbd>) under **Next day starts at**. That and **Desired
retention**, how likely you should be to remember a card when it comes due, belong to the
collection: they're kept in its `.amgi.sqlite`, so they move with the folder and the
[`amgi` command](/cli) uses them too.

**New cards per day** in Settings sets how many cards you haven't seen before come up each
day, 20 unless you change it. Set it to 0 to only review what you've already learned.
The limit covers the whole collection; deck counts and `amgi status` show what's left of it.

To keep a day's work in check, turn on **Limit reviews per day** in Settings and choose
how many. Once you've reviewed that many due cards, the rest wait for the next day, most
overdue first, and the due counts stop there. Cards you forgot that day still come back,
and new cards have their own limit; neither counts toward the limit. Like the
settings above, both limits belong to the collection. A low limit lets a backlog grow,
so raise it again once you've caught up.

## Leeches

A card you keep forgetting is a **leech**: it usually needs rewording or splitting more
than it needs more reviews. Once you've forgotten a card 8 times, a note beside the next
card says so, and again every 4 times after that. Change the number with **Leech
threshold** in Settings; the notes then come every half threshold. Turn on **Suspend
leeches** to have Amgi also [suspend](/card-format#suspending-cards) a card when it
becomes a leech, by tagging it `#suspended` in its file. <kbd>⌘Z</kbd> takes back the
answer and the suspension together. Both settings belong to the collection.
`amgi search is:leech` lists your leeches.

The **Retention** table in `amgi stats` shows how much you
remembered today, yesterday, and over the last week, month and year: the share of reviews
not rated **Again**, counting only cards that were scheduled a day or more ahead, as
Anki does, so a card you forgot isn't counted again when it comes back. It's split into
young and mature cards by that interval: under 21 days is young. Compare
it with your desired retention; if it's lower, cards are coming back too late.

## Time spent studying

Amgi times each card from when it shows until you rate it. The timer pauses whenever you
can't be studying: while another app is in front, the window is minimized or
covered, or the screen is asleep or locked. A card counts a minute at most, in case you
step away with it on screen, as Anki does by default. If you take back an answer with
<kbd>⌘Z</kbd>, the card's timer carries on from where it was.

- `amgi stats` shows the time studied today, about how long the cards left today will
  take, and the average time per day you studied and per review over the last 30 days.
  `amgi stats --time` shades the days by time instead of reviews, and its forecast says
  about how long each day's due cards will take.
- **Card Info** (<kbd>⌘I</kbd>) shows a card's average and total time; hover a review for its own.
- The header and the menu bar show the time left today, and the menu bar the
  time studied so far.

The estimates go by your last 30 days: new cards by how long a new card took on the day
you first studied it, Again and repeats included, and due cards by how long one took on
the day it came back. Reviews from before Amgi timed them have no time, so days before
then show their reviews but no time. [Anki imports](/anki-import) bring their times along.

## Adding cards

**Add Card** (<kbd>⌥⌘N</kbd> from any app, or <kbd>⌘N</kbd> in Amgi) opens a small panel
over whatever you're doing: pick a deck, type a question and answer or a cloze, and press
<kbd>⌘↩</kbd>. The card is appended to the end of the deck's file, which is created if
needed. <kbd>Return</kbd> moves to the next field and <kbd>⌥↩</kbd> starts a new line; <kbd>Esc</kbd> puts the panel away and
keeps what you typed. Turn the shortcut off in **Settings**.

Next to Basic and Cloze, the menu at the top lists your
[note types](/card-format#notes-with-fields); a deck with notes starts with the type
most of them have. For a note, the panel shows a box for each field and says how many
cards the note makes; fields left empty are left out. Drop an image or sound onto a
field to copy it into the collection's `media` folder and link it there.

## Menu bar

The menu bar shows how many cards are due or being learned. Its menu lists what's left today
and about how long it will take, and the reviews and [time](#time-spent-studying) so far;
pick **Study Now** to start reviewing. While it's shown, Amgi keeps running when its window is closed.
Hide it in **Settings** (<kbd>⌘,</kbd>).
